Short Answer Question
3 parts (a, b, c)  ·  No thesis required  ·  Suggested: 40 minutes  ·  20% of exam score

Stimulus & Prompt

"The period from 1450 to 1750 witnessed a dramatic expansion of global trade networks, facilitated by European maritime exploration. These networks transferred not only goods but also people, plants, animals, and diseases across previously isolated regions, transforming societies on every inhabited continent."


Using the passage and your knowledge of world history, answer parts (a), (b), and (c).


(a) Briefly describe ONE specific historical development that contributed to the expansion of global trade networks in the period 1450–1750 referenced in the passage.


(b) Briefly explain ONE specific effect of the expansion of global trade networks on a society outside of Europe in the period 1450–1750.


(c) Briefly explain ONE way the expansion of global trade networks in the period 1450–1750 represents a continuity with trade patterns from the period 1200–1450.

Official Rubric (3 points total — 1 per part): Each response must include a specific, accurate historical development or explanation — not a restatement of the prompt. General claims without specific evidence earn no credit.

Document-Based Question
Thesis + argument using 6 of 7 documents  ·  Suggested: 60 minutes  ·  25% of exam score

Prompt

Evaluate the extent to which the Industrial Revolution transformed social and economic structures in the period 1750–1900.


Documents

Document 1 — Andrew Ure, The Philosophy of Manufactures, 1835 (British factory owner):
"The factory system raises the condition of all who participate in its benefits. Children in factories are treated with great kindness and given regular meals and a structured routine that improves their moral character."


Document 2 — Parliamentary testimony of an English mill worker, 1832:
"I have worked in the mill since I was seven years old. We begin at five in the morning and work until eight at night. The overlooker beats us with a strap if we fall asleep at the machines. My legs are bent from standing so long."


Document 3 — Friedrich Engels, The Condition of the Working Class in England, 1845:
"Workers live in filth, crowded into dark, damp cellars. The great industrial towns are sinks of human suffering — workers never see sunlight or breathe clean air. Disease, despair, and early death are the inevitable results of the factory system."


Document 4 — Edwin Chadwick, Report on the Sanitary Conditions of the Labouring Population, 1842 (British social reformer):
"The annual loss of life from filth and poor ventilation in industrial districts exceeds the loss of life from any wars in which Britain has engaged in modern times. Disease follows directly and inevitably from the conditions under which industrial laborers live and work."


Document 5 — Samuel Smiles, Self-Help, 1859 (British author):
"The spirit of self-help is the root of all genuine growth in the individual. The road to advancement lies open to all — industry and perseverance, not birth or privilege, determine a man's place in society. The factory offers opportunity to any man willing to work."


Document 6 — Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels, The Communist Manifesto, 1848:
"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles. The bourgeoisie has stripped of its halo every occupation hitherto honored. It has left remaining no other bond between man and man than naked self-interest, callous cash payment."


Document 7 — Japanese government commission report on Western industrialization, 1890:
"The Western nations have grown rich and powerful through adoption of machinery and the factory system. Japan must follow this path without delay or risk becoming a subject nation of the industrial powers. There is no alternative to industrialization if Japan is to remain sovereign."

Official Rubric (7 points):
Thesis (1): Historically defensible claim + line of reasoning beyond restating the prompt.
Contextualization (1): Accurate broader historical context meaningfully connected to argument.
Document Evidence (2): Content of ≥3 docs to address topic (1pt); ≥6 docs supporting argument (2pts).
Outside Evidence (1): Specific evidence beyond the documents supporting the argument.
Sourcing/HAPP (1): Historical situation, audience, purpose, or point of view explained for ≥3 docs.
Complexity (1): Corroboration, qualification, or explaining multiple causes/effects/continuities.
