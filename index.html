Short Answer Question
3 parts (a, b, c) No thesis required ~40 min suggested 20% of exam score

Stimulus & Prompt

"The period from 1450 to 1750 witnessed a dramatic expansion of global trade networks, facilitated by European maritime exploration. These networks transferred not only goods but also people, plants, animals, and diseases across previously isolated regions, transforming societies on every inhabited continent."


Using the passage and your knowledge of world history, answer parts (a), (b), and (c).


(a) Briefly describe ONE specific historical development that contributed to the expansion of global trade networks in the period 1450–1750.


(b) Briefly explain ONE specific effect of the expansion of global trade networks on a society outside of Europe in the period 1450–1750.


(c) Briefly explain ONE way the expansion of global trade networks in the period 1450–1750 represents a continuity with trade patterns from 1200–1450.

Official Rubric — 3 pts (1 per part): Each response must include a specific, historically accurate development or explanation. General claims without specific evidence earn no credit.

Document-Based Question
Use 6 of 7 documents ~60 min suggested 25% of exam score

Prompt

Evaluate the extent to which the Industrial Revolution transformed social and economic structures in the period 1750–1900.


Documents

Doc 1 — Andrew Ure, The Philosophy of Manufactures, 1835: "The factory system raises the condition of all who participate in its benefits. Children in factories are treated with great kindness and given regular meals and a structured routine."


Doc 2 — Parliamentary testimony, English mill worker, 1832: "I have worked in the mill since I was seven. We begin at five in the morning and work until eight at night. The overlooker beats us if we fall asleep."


Doc 3 — Friedrich Engels, The Condition of the Working Class in England, 1845: "Workers live in filth, crowded into dark damp cellars. The great industrial towns are sinks of human suffering."


Doc 4 — Edwin Chadwick, Report on Sanitary Conditions, 1842: "The annual loss of life from filth and poor ventilation in industrial districts exceeds the loss from any wars in which Britain has engaged in modern times."


Doc 5 — Samuel Smiles, Self-Help, 1859: "The spirit of self-help is the root of all genuine growth. The road to advancement lies open to all — industry and perseverance determine a man's station."


Doc 6 — Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels, The Communist Manifesto, 1848: "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles. The bourgeoisie has left no bond between man and man than naked self-interest."


Doc 7 — Japanese government commission report, 1890: "The Western nations have grown powerful through adoption of machinery and the factory system. Japan must industrialize or risk becoming a colony of the industrial powers."

Official Rubric — 7 pts:
Thesis (1) — Defensible claim + line of reasoning.
Contextualization (1) — Broader historical context meaningfully connected.
Document Evidence (2) — ≥3 docs address topic (1pt); ≥6 docs support argument (2pts).
Outside Evidence (1) — Specific evidence beyond the documents.
Sourcing/HAPP (1) — Historical situation, audience, purpose, or POV for ≥3 docs.
Complexity (1) — Corroboration, qualification, or multiple causes/effects.

Free Response — Long FRQ
Source: 2022 AP Bio Exam · FRQ #1 ~25 min suggested 8–10 pts

Prompt — 2022 AP Biology Free-Response Question 1

Glucose is a primary source of energy for cellular metabolism. In eukaryotic cells, glucose is broken down through a series of metabolic pathways.


(a) Glycolysis is the first stage of cellular respiration. Identify the location where glycolysis occurs in a eukaryotic cell and describe what happens to the pyruvate produced during glycolysis when oxygen is available.


(b) The citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) produces electron carriers. Identify two electron carriers produced by the citric acid cycle and explain how these carriers contribute to ATP synthesis in the electron transport chain.


(c) A researcher blocks Complex I of the electron transport chain using a poison. Predict and explain the effect of this treatment on: (i) the proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane, and (ii) the overall ATP yield per glucose molecule.


(d) Some organisms can survive in the absence of oxygen. Describe the process of lactic acid fermentation and explain why it is necessary for continued ATP production under anaerobic conditions.

Official Rubric (~10 pts): Location of glycolysis + fate of pyruvate with O₂ (2), Two electron carriers + role in ETC/ATP synthesis (2), Effect on proton gradient + ATP yield with reasoning (3), Fermentation description + necessity for glycolysis continuity (2), Scientific communication (1).

Free Response — Experimental Design FRQ
Source: 2019 AP Bio Exam · FRQ #2 ~22 min suggested 6 pts

Prompt — 2019 AP Biology Free-Response Question 2

Pea plants produce flowers that can be either purple or white. A student performs crosses between true-breeding purple-flowered plants and true-breeding white-flowered plants.


Cross 1: Purple ✕ White → All F₁ offspring are purple-flowered.
Cross 2: F₁ ✕ F₁ → F₂ offspring are approximately 3 purple : 1 white.


(a) Based on the data above, identify which flower color is dominant and provide evidence from the crosses to support your claim.


(b) Using appropriate genetic notation, write the genotypes of the true-breeding purple parent, the true-breeding white parent, and the F₁ offspring. Explain your reasoning.


(c) A student observes that an F₂ purple-flowered plant, when self-crossed, produces only purple-flowered offspring. Predict the genotype of this F₂ plant and explain how this result supports your prediction.


(d) The gene for flower color is located on chromosome 1. The gene for plant height (tall vs. short) is located on chromosome 7. Predict whether these two genes will assort independently, and identify the law that supports your prediction.

Official Rubric (~6 pts): Dominant/recessive identification with evidence (1), Correct genotypes with reasoning (2), Homozygous dominant genotype + explanation (1), Independent assortment + Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment (2).

Free Response — Concept Application
Scenario-based FRQ ~25 min suggested 7 pts

Scenario

Maya is a college student who recently started a new job and is having trouble remembering her training. She notices she can easily recall information she studied just before sleeping but forgets material studied in the middle of the day. She also finds that stressful situations make it harder to concentrate.


Using your knowledge of psychology, answer the following:


(a) Explain how encoding specificity might help Maya remember her training material better.


(b) Explain the serial position effect that could account for her better recall of material studied before sleeping.


(c) Describe how cortisol released during stress affects her memory and concentration.


(d) Maya's manager suggests using spaced practice instead of massed practice. Explain why this would benefit Maya's long-term retention.


(e) Identify the brain structure most central to forming new explicit memories and explain its role.

Scoring Guide (7 pts, 1–2 per part): Each answer must correctly define the concept AND apply it to Maya's situation. Definitional answers without application earn partial credit only.

Free Response — Research Methods
Research design FRQ ~20 min suggested 7 pts

Scenario

A researcher wants to test whether listening to classical music improves performance on math tests. She randomly assigns 60 participants into two groups: one group listens to Mozart for 10 minutes before a math test; the other group sits in silence for 10 minutes before the same test.


(a) Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable in this study.


(b) Explain why random assignment is important in this experiment.


(c) The researcher finds that the Mozart group scored 8% higher on average. Explain whether this result would allow her to establish cause and effect.


(d) A classmate suggests that the results might be explained by the placebo effect. Explain what this means in this context.


(e) Describe ONE ethical principle from the APA guidelines that the researcher must follow in this study.

Scoring Guide (7 pts): Correct IV/DV identification (2), Purpose of random assignment (1), Causation reasoning (1), Placebo effect application (1), APA ethical principle (2).

Free Response — Polynomial Functions
Source: 2024 AP Precalculus Exam · FRQ #1 🚫 No Calculator ~30 min suggested 6 pts

Prompt — 2024 AP Precalculus Free-Response Question 1 (No Calculator)

The polynomial function p is given by p(x) = −(x + 1)(x − 1)(x − 3)².


(a) The polynomial function q is given by q(x) = (x + 1)(x − 1)(x − 3)². Describe how the graphs of p and q are related. Explain why this is the case in terms of the leading coefficient.


(b) Determine and interpret the end behavior of p(x) as x → +∞ and as x → −∞. Explain how the degree and leading coefficient determine the end behavior.


(c) The function p has zeros at x = −1, x = 1, and x = 3. Determine whether the graph of p crosses or only touches (but does not cross) the x-axis at each zero, and explain your reasoning using the multiplicity of each zero.


(d) The graph of p has a local maximum between x = −1 and x = 1. Identify the approximate x-coordinate of the local maximum, explaining your reasoning. Then identify all intervals over which p is increasing.

Official Rubric (6 pts): Relationship between p and q with leading coefficient reasoning (1), Correct end behavior with explanation (2), Zero behavior with multiplicity reasoning for all three zeros (2), Local max identification + increasing intervals (1).

Free Response — Sinusoidal Functions & Modeling
Source: 2024 AP Precalculus Exam · FRQ #2 ✅ Calculator Active ~30 min suggested 6 pts

Prompt — 2024 AP Precalculus Free-Response Question 2 (Calculator)

The depth of water, in feet, at a coastal location can be modeled by a sinusoidal function. The table below gives the depth of water at selected times during one day.


Time (hours after midnight): 0 → depth 6.0 ft | 3 → depth 11.5 ft | 6 → depth 6.0 ft | 9 → depth 0.5 ft | 12 → depth 6.0 ft


(a) Determine the amplitude, midline, and period of a sinusoidal function that models the depth of water d(t) based on the data in the table. Explain how you determined each value.


(b) Write an equation for d(t), the depth of water in feet, as a function of t, the number of hours after midnight. Show your work.


(c) According to your model, what is the depth of water at t = 4 hours? Show your work and round to the nearest hundredth.


(d) A boat requires a minimum water depth of 8 feet to dock safely. Using your model, determine all time intervals during the first 12 hours after midnight when the boat can dock. Show the algebraic or graphical work that supports your answer. Round to the nearest hundredth.

Official Rubric (6 pts): Amplitude, midline, period all correct with explanations (2), Correct sinusoidal equation (1), Depth at t=4 with work (1), Time intervals when d ≥ 8 with supporting work (2).
